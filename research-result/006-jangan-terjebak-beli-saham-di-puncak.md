Why do retail investors love buying at the top and how can we avoid getting trapped? Let me explain it to you like you're in elementary school. If you do this often, you need to learn about human behavior. There is a theory called the Informational Cascade. The logic is like this: if you see a stock being bought by many people, surely all those people can't be wrong. So you start abandoning your analysis and follow the majority's decision. Yet, that majority decision could be the result of people making choices because others did. So it is not really the result of independent analysis. That's why I want to share a few ways we can avoid getting trapped buying at the top . The first and most important thing is , you have to ask yourself. What is your thesis for entering? Why are you getting in? Are you looking at the macro side? Are you looking at the business fundamentals? Is the valuation attractive, or are you looking at the momentum? You must have your own thesis , not just follow what other people say . Now, second, if I personally enter a stock, I will enter slowly, aka I will scale in. So, I don't go all-in upfront , but I'll do it more gradually. Why is that? Because in my experience, if we make buying decisions over several days , our brains are more rational. We are less FOMO-driven, and our mindset is more focused on longer-term thinking. So if along the way we feel, okay, maybe our accumulation time needs to be faster or slower because there are many other theses to consider. That's ok. We get to have many options to think about . Plus, if we buy gradually, we can get a more attractive average price. The third thing I usually do is check whether retail investors are starting to enter or not. Because there's a saying that retail often buys at the top. That means we just need to do the opposite of retail. So if I see brokers affiliated with retail money like XL, X , or Y. I usually notice if they are buying into a stock collectively. It could mean they might become the exit liquidity for smart money that entered earlier. Although, a disclaimer: actually, buying at the top is not always wrong. Because every stock that hits an all-time high will definitely form peak after peak. So it's actually fine to buy at the top as long as you aren't trapped at a peak that eventually flatlines at 50. But how about you, what is your own strategy like? Let me know in the comments.
